# Doctoral course syllabus

Last verified: 2026-09-05

**Title:** Agentic AI Security, Identity, and Access Control: A Gap-Finding Reading & Project Course  
**Candidate:** Aditya Sissodiya  
**Cycle:** Third-cycle (doctoral)  
**Credits:** 7.5 ECTS (7.5 hp), approximately 200 hours  
**Format:** Individual reading and project course  
**Primary assessment:** A submittable paper-shaped artifact with a reproducible prototype or evaluation design

## Intended learning outcomes

### Knowledge and understanding

1. Account, at the research frontier, for the architecture of agentic systems and the authorization stories of MCP, A2A, ACP, and their governance context.
2. Explain the state of the art and its limits in agent identity, delegation semantics, and policy/enforcement models.
3. Situate agent authorization within classical access-control theory: HRU safety, take-grant, and trust-management decidability.

### Skills and abilities

4. Critically appraise a fast-moving, grey-literature-heavy field while separating peer-reviewed results, standards, and vendor claims.
5. Threat-model a multi-agent, multi-stakeholder scenario with OWASP ASI01–ASI10, T01–T17, and MAESTRO, mapping threats to candidate mechanisms.
6. Implement and empirically probe a small delegation/authorization mechanism and state what a valid evaluation requires.

### Judgement and approach

7. Formulate, defend, and prioritize research gaps while honestly assessing concurrent work.
8. Produce a submittable, venue-appropriate paper artifact and appraise the ethical and societal stakes of delegated autonomous authority.

## Assessment

| Component | Weight | Evidence |
|---|---:|---|
| Paper-shaped artifact | 60% | Preferred: G2+G1 SoK with DPP/G7 instantiation; alternative position paper + prototype |
| Six seminar lead-discussions | 15% | Briefs and discussion notes |
| Seven module exercises | 15% | Checkable outputs in `modules/` and `labs/` |
| Oral defense | 10% | Reviewer-style questions and revision log |

The pass/fail gate is correct separation of established results, standards, inferences, and open problems. The artifact must demonstrate primary-source command, a defensible gap taxonomy, a coherent contribution, and reproducible or honestly bounded evaluation.
